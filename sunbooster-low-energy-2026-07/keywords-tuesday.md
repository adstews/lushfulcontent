## What we deliberately did NOT buy (and why the client should care)

| Family | Volume | Why excluded |
|---|---|---|
| vitamin d lamp / light / uvb | ~11,000/mo | SunBooster emits 850nm NIR — **no UVB, so it can't make vitamin D**. Honest brands don't buy intent they can't serve; it would also convert terribly. |
| sad lamp / seasonal depression lamps | ~7,000/mo | Medical-condition territory — Google health-ad risk (this account has old disapprovals from 2025 sunlight campaigns) and a claim the brand can't make. |
| "why am i so sleepy" cluster | ~10,000/mo | Sleep-disorder adjacency, weaker fit than "tired/energy". Phase 2 if the tired cluster wins. |
| verilux / sperti / philips wake-up light | ~8,600/mo | Competitor conquesting — a clean phase-2 test, kept out so the first read isn't muddied. |
| morning light therapy | 170/mo | Rejected by Google's "Birth Control" policy classifier (it reads "morning" as morning-after pill). Yes, really. |

Plus a **76-term negative list**: solar/garden lights, grow lights, pets/reptiles, tanning/UV, medical
conditions (depression, thyroid, anemia...), supplements & energy drinks, song lyrics/reddit,
light bulbs, competitor brands, vitamin d.

## Same thesis on Amazon PPC (ready to port)

For Sponsored Products (manual): Amazon shoppers search products, not symptoms — so the "low energy"
angle ports as the *solution* vocabulary:

- **Exact:** sun lamp, sunlight lamp, happy light, light therapy lamp, sun lamp for office, desk sun
  lamp, artificial sunlight lamp, near infrared light device
- **Phrase:** light for energy, energy lamp, sunlight alternative, infrared light therapy
- **Negatives:** solar, grow, plant, reptile, tanning, uvb, bulb, sad (same honesty rule: skip
  vitamin d terms)
- The symptom angle ("tired at your desk") belongs in the **listing creative/A+ content** and
  Sponsored Brands headline copy rather than keywords — Amazon queries with symptom language are near
  zero.

## How we'll judge it (no Google conversions will show — by design)

All ads land on Amazon, so Google's conversion column stays 0. The scoreboard is: clicks + CPC + CTR
on the Google side, and **Amazon Attribution** (clicks → detail-page views → purchases) on the Amazon
side. First read after ~5-7 days of spend.
